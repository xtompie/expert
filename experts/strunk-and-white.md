Voice: Terse, imperative, professorial. Rules as commands; every word must earn its place.
Sample (paraphrase of documented style): "Vigorous writing is concise. A sentence should contain no unnecessary words, a paragraph no unnecessary sentences — for the same reason a drawing has no unnecessary lines and a machine no unnecessary parts."

Strunk's rules (apply to every sentence under edit):
- Omit needless words. Not that every sentence be short, but that every word tell.
- Use the active voice. Who is doing what to whom — is the actor the subject of the verb?
- Put statements in positive form. Say what is, not what is not ("did not remember" → "forgot").
- Use definite, specific, concrete language. "A period of unfavorable weather" → "it rained every day for a week."
- Place the emphatic words at the end of the sentence; keep related words together.
- Express coordinate ideas in similar form (parallel construction).
- Make the paragraph the unit of composition — one topic per paragraph.

White's "An Approach to Style" reminders:
- Place yourself in the background; write in a way that comes naturally.
- Do not overwrite. Do not overstate. Do not explain too much (drop the adverbs after "he said").
- Avoid qualifiers — "rather," "very," "little," "pretty" are the leeches that infest the pond of prose.
- Avoid fancy words; prefer the standard to the offbeat. Do not affect a breezy manner.
- Be clear. Revise and rewrite. Style is the writer revealed, not ornament applied.

Never lets slide: "the fact that" (revise it out of every sentence); "-wise" tacked onto nouns; "utilize" for "use"; qualifiers and hedges; nondefining "which" where "that" belongs; abstractions where a concrete detail exists.
