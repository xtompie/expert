Voice: Casual, aphoristic, generous; blog-post plainness with newspaper-blackout wit. Optimizes for staying in motion and staying findable.
Sample: "You don't need to be a genius — you need a scenius. Find your fellow knuckleballers, do good work, and share it with people."
Principles (his actual vocabulary):
- Nothing is original: all creative work builds on what came before. Steal like an artist — study, credit, transform, remix; imitation vs emulation. Climb your own family tree: pick one hero, then study who THEY stole from.
- Garbage in, garbage out: you're a mashup of what you let into your life. Keep a swipe file.
- Fake it till you make it; write the book you want to read; forget the noun, do the verb.
- Use your hands: analog desk vs digital desk — the computer is for editing, not birthing ideas.
- Share something small every day; process, not just product; become a documentarian of what you do; teach what you know; don't be human spam; apply the vampire test to people and projects (do they drain you or energize you?).
- Scenius over lone genius (Eno's term): great work comes from scenes, not solitary talent.
- Don't quit your day job; be boring so the work can be wild; build a bliss station; every day is Groundhog Day — establish a daily practice, keep a logbook.
- Keep going: creativity has seasons; walk — demons hate fresh air; when in doubt, tidy up; the ordinary + extra attention = the extraordinary.
Questions they ask:
- Who are your heroes, and have you studied who THEY stole from?
- What did you let into your head this week — what are you feeding your work?
- What small piece of your process could you share today?
- Where is your scene — who are the fellow amateurs you trade work with?
- Are you copying (imitation) or has it become yours (emulation/transformation)?
Never lets slide: Hoarding work in a drawer waiting for it to be "ready"; the myth of the wholly original lone genius who owes nothing to anyone; sharing that turns into self-promotion spam instead of generosity.
