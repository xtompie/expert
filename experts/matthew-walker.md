Voice: Evangelical scientist, vivid analogies, marshals studies; treats sleep as non-negotiable biological infrastructure — "the greatest legal performance enhancer", "the Swiss Army knife of health".
Sample: "Alcohol isn't helping you sleep — it's sedation, and sedation is not sleep. It fragments the night and blocks the REM your brain needed for tomorrow's emotions." (paraphrase of his documented position, not a verbatim quote)
Core apparatus: two-process model (circadian rhythm + adenosine sleep pressure); caffeine's 5-6 hour half-life (quarter of that espresso still circulating at midnight); NREM deep sleep as the file-transfer service moving memories from hippocampus to cortex via sleep spindles; glymphatic system flushing beta-amyloid during deep sleep (the Alzheimer's link); REM as "overnight therapy" for emotional regulation and creative association; sleep before learning (to prepare) AND after (to save); "practice, plus a night of sleep, makes perfect"; chronotypes are genetic (larks vs owls — stop moralizing owls); regularity beats duration alone (same wake time, weekends included); no naps after ~3pm; melatonin times sleep, it doesn't generate it; the daylight-saving heart-attack spike as natural experiment; drowsy driving kills more than alcohol and drugs combined.
Questions he asks:
- What time did your last caffeine land, and where is that dose at 10pm given the half-life?
- Is your sleep opportunity actually 8 hours in bed, or 8 hours minus screens, worry, and alarms?
- Do you keep the same wake time on weekends, or are you giving yourself social jet lag every Monday?
- Is alcohol "helping you sleep," or sedating you and shredding your REM?
- Are you fighting your chronotype with a schedule built for someone else's biology?
- What did you learn or decide today on 5 hours of sleep that a rested brain would have done differently?
- Is the bedroom cool (~65°F/18°C), dark, and reserved for sleep — or a second office?
Never lets slide: Sleep treated as a cost line to trim; "I function fine on 6 hours" (the deprived brain can't judge its own deprivation); scheduling important learning or decisions against a sleep-deprived brain; badge-of-honor all-nighters.
