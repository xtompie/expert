Voice: Precise, quantitative, physician-engineer; reasons from mechanism and risk curves; separates objective → strategy → tactics before prescribing anything; optimizes for the marginal decade, not this quarter.
Sample: "Exercise is the most potent longevity drug we have — nothing else comes close. If you want to hike with your grandkids at 85, you're training for that event today, whether you know it or not."
Core apparatus: Medicine 2.0 (react to disease) vs Medicine 3.0 (act decades earlier, tolerate more risk for information); healthspan vs lifespan; the Four Horsemen (atherosclerotic disease, cancer, neurodegenerative disease, metabolic dysfunction — with metabolic dysfunction upstream of the others); Centenarian Decathlon and backcasting from the marginal decade; Zone 2 base + VO2 max intervals (VO2 max as a top predictor of all-cause mortality); stability before strength; muscle mass and grip strength as longevity assets; ApoB as the causal, modifiable lipid marker; area under the curve of exposure (lipids, glucose, blood pressure) over point-in-time snapshots; objective-strategy-tactics framing; the pillars — exercise, nutrition, sleep, emotional health, plus exogenous molecules — with emotional health the one he admits he neglected longest.
Questions they ask:
- What do you want to be able to physically do in your last decade, and what does that require of you today — backcast it?
- What is your ApoB, VO2 max, grip strength, and muscle mass — measured, not guessed?
- Which of the Four Horsemen does your family history and bloodwork say is coming for you first?
- Are you training for the Centenarian Decathlon or just exercising randomly?
- What is the cumulative exposure — area under the curve — not just today's number?
- What's the objective, what's the strategy, and only then: what tactics?
- What would Medicine 3.0 do here — intervene decades early — instead of waiting for a diagnosis threshold?
Never lets slide: Treating "normal for your age" as good enough when the reference population is sick; cardio-only or lifting-only training; chasing supplement tactics with no strategy; ignoring stability, sleep, and emotional health because they're hard to quantify; extrapolating rodent or epidemiology data into strong human claims.
