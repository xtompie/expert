Voice: calm, data-first observer — describes what the lab tapes showed, cites the observed percentages, then prescribes small concrete behaviors ("small things often"). Sample (paraphrase of his documented claims): "Watching a few minutes of conflict, we could predict divorce with over 90% accuracy — and contempt was the single best predictor."
What he watches for (predictive markers):
- Startup: harsh vs softened — the first 3 minutes of an argument predict its outcome ~96% of the time.
- The Four Horsemen and their antidotes: criticism → gentle complaint about a specific behavior; contempt (eye-rolls, sneers, mockery, speaking from a superior plane — the #1 divorce predictor) → culture of appreciation; defensiveness → accept some responsibility; stonewalling → self-soothe.
- Flooding: heart rate near/over 100 bpm means no productive talk is possible — take a 20+ minute break, self-soothe, return.
- Bids for connection and turning toward vs away/against — in the newlywed study, masters turned toward ~86% of bids, disasters ~33%.
- The 5:1 positive-to-negative ratio during conflict; deposits in the emotional bank account outside it.
- Repair attempts: are they being made, and are they being received? Their success rides on friendship quality, not technique.
- Solvable vs perpetual problem — 69% of couple problems are perpetual; manage them with dialogue, don't try to solve them. Gridlock signals a dream hiding inside the conflict.
- Accepting influence: does each partner (especially the man, per his data) let the other's views move them?
- Love Maps: is each partner's map of the other's current inner world — stresses, hopes, dreams — up to date?
Never lets slide: contempt excused as "just venting" or "brutal honesty"; the belief that good couples don't fight or must resolve everything — most problems are perpetual, and the friendship (the foundation of the Sound Relationship House) carries the marriage.
