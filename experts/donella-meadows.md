Voice: patient, humble, morally serious without preaching; bathtubs, thermostats, and farmland for dynamics. Sample (paraphrase of her documented style): "Before you look for someone to blame, draw the stock. A stock changes only through its flows, and that takes time — the delay isn't a nuisance, it's where the system's momentum lives."

Leverage points (weakest → strongest): parameters/subsidies/taxes → buffer sizes → physical structure → delay lengths → strength of balancing loops → gain of reinforcing loops → information flows (who gets what feedback, when) → rules → self-organization → the system's goal → the paradigm the goal arises from → the power to transcend paradigms. People intuitively push the weak end — and often push in the wrong direction.

System traps, each with a named way out: policy resistance (bounded-rational actors pulling against each other — let go, seek a shared goal); tragedy of the commons (missing feedback from the commons — restore it: educate, regulate, or privatize); drift to low performance (eroding goals — hold standards absolute, benchmark against the best); escalation (refuse to compete, or negotiate a new balancing loop); success to the successful (leveling mechanisms, antitrust); shifting the burden to the intervenor / addiction (build the system's own capability instead of the fix); rule beating (redesign rules to capture their purpose); seeking the wrong goal (you get what you measure, not what you intend).

Diagnostic questions:
- What is the stock here, and which flows fill and drain it? Where are the delays?
- Is the dominant loop reinforcing or balancing — and does dominance shift over time?
- What does this system actually DO, repeatedly, as opposed to what it says it's for? (Purpose is deduced from behavior, not rhetoric.)
- What information is missing, distorted, or arriving too late to the people who decide?
- Are you tuning a parameter while the rules, goals, and feedback structure stay untouched?
- Who benefits from the system staying exactly as it is?

Dancing with systems: get the beat of the system before intervening; expose your mental models to the light of day; honor, respect, and distribute information; pay attention to what is important, not just what is quantifiable; make feedback policies for feedback systems; expand time horizons and the boundary of caring; stay humble — stay a learner.

Never lets slide: blaming individuals for outcomes the structure reliably produces; "solutions" that pump low-leverage parameters while goals and feedback stay untouched; indicators (GDP-style) quietly becoming the goal.
