A crucial conversation = high stakes + opposing opinions + strong emotions. Core bet: results are set by the pool of shared meaning, and safety — not content — is what keeps meaning flowing. When people feel unsafe they don't get quiet about the topic; they get quiet (or hostile) about you.

Procedure — the skill sequence, in order:
1. Start with Heart — work on me first. What do I really want for me, for them, for the relationship? Refuse the Fool's Choice (honest OR kind — find the AND).
2. Learn to Look — spot the moment it turns crucial: physical signs, then silence (masking, avoiding, withdrawing) or violence (controlling, labeling, attacking).
3. Make It Safe — step OUT of content; restore Mutual Purpose and Mutual Respect. Contrasting fixes misunderstanding ("I don't mean X. I do mean Y."). CRIB builds mutual purpose (Commit to seek it, Recognize the purpose behind the strategy, Invent a mutual purpose, Brainstorm new strategies).
4. Master My Stories — retrace the Path to Action backwards (act ← feel ← story ← see/hear). Name the clever stories: Victim ("not my fault"), Villain ("all their fault"), Helpless ("nothing else I can do"). Ask: what am I pretending not to notice about my role?
5. STATE My Path — Share facts first, Tell your story, Ask for others' paths, Talk tentatively ("I'm beginning to wonder whether…"), Encourage testing (invite disagreement and mean it).
6. Explore Others' Paths — AMPP: Ask, Mirror, Paraphrase, Prime. When views differ: Agree where you do, Build on it, Compare where you don't.
7. Move to Action — decide how to decide (command / consult / vote / consensus), then who does what by when, and how you'll follow up.

Diagnostic questions:
- Is it safe to talk right now — and are they sliding toward silence or violence?
- What story am I telling myself, and what are the bare facts underneath it?
- What's missing from the pool of shared meaning, and how does it get in?

Never lets slide: leading with conclusions instead of facts; sacrificing safety to win the point; treating sugarcoating vs. brutal honesty as the only options; conversations that end warm and agreeable but with no who-does-what-by-when.
