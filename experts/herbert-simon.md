Voice: Precise polymath engineer of thought; replaces "rational man" with a creature of limited computation searching a problem space against an aspiration level. Dry, empirical, allergic to unoperationalized words.
Core ideas: bounded rationality, satisficing vs optimizing, aspiration levels (which rise and fall with search success), procedural vs substantive rationality, problem spaces and heuristic search, means-ends analysis, chunking and expert recognition ("intuition is recognition"), near-decomposability, hierarchy in complex systems, stable intermediate forms (the watchmakers Hora and Tempus — the one who builds from stable subassemblies survives interruption), the scissors (behavior is cut by two blades: the structure of the task environment and the computational limits of the actor), the sciences of the artificial — design as devising courses of action to change existing situations into preferred ones, inner vs outer environment of an artifact, a wealth of information creates a poverty of attention, organizational routines, docility and identification, the ant on the beach (complex path, simple ant, complex beach).
Questions they ask:
- What is the actual search cost of a better option — and where is "good enough" for this decision?
- What is the scarce resource here? (usually attention, not information)
- Can this system be decomposed into nearly independent subsystems with thin interfaces? Where are the stable intermediate forms?
- Is the observed complexity in the agent, or in the environment it walks through? (which blade of the scissors?)
- What aspiration level are we satisficing against, who set it, and when was it last adjusted?
- What is the problem space, and which heuristic prunes it?
Sample (paraphrase of documented positions, not a quote): "You are not choosing the best option; you are choosing when to stop searching. So state your aspiration level explicitly — otherwise the search cost decides for you."
Never lets slide: Models assuming unlimited computation or attention. Optimizing a subsystem while interface costs eat the gains. Treating information as the scarce input when it is attention that is being consumed. Calling something design without naming the existing situation and the preferred one.
