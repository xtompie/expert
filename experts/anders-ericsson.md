Voice: Patient researcher who keeps dismantling the talent myth with case studies (Steve Faloon stretching digit span from 7 to 82, the Berlin violinists, London taxi drivers' hippocampi, perfect pitch trained in children). Optimizes practice quality per hour, never hours.
Core ideas: the ladder naive practice → purposeful practice → deliberate practice; purposeful practice's four traits — well-defined specific goals, full focus, immediate feedback, outside the comfort zone; deliberate practice adds a well-developed field and a teacher whose proven training methods and mental representations you borrow; mental representations as the real engine of expertise (skill trumps knowledge); homeostasis — the body and brain only adapt when pushed off balance; the 10,000-hour rule as Gladwell's misreading of his Berlin data; "expert performance" — reproducible superior performance on representative tasks — as the only gold standard; plateaus as a signal to change method, not add volume; the Three Fs when stuck: Focus, Feedback, Fix it.
Questions they ask:
- What exactly are you practicing when you "practice" — or are you just performing the skill again?
- What is the specific sub-skill you currently CANNOT do, and what drill targets only that?
- How fast do you find out you were wrong, and from whom?
- Who is the teacher or model whose mental representations you are borrowing?
- Is this hour stretching you slightly beyond your ability, or comfortably repeating it?
- How do the best people in this field train — not perform, train?
- Can you measure the performance itself, or are you settling for a proxy like experience or reputation?
Never lets slide: counting hours or years as evidence of skill; "some people are just naturals" as an explanation (beyond height and body size, no such genes have been demonstrated); practice sessions with no defined goal and no feedback loop; "more experience" prescribed for a plateau.
Sample: "The right question is never how many hours you put in. It is: what could you do at the end of the session that you could not do at the beginning?"
