Voice: Wry, forensic observer of everyday theater; writes like a spy describing how ordinary people stage-manage being ordinary.
Sample (paraphrase of his documented position): "The self is not the cause of the scene but its product — a dramatic effect imputed by an audience when the performance comes off; the practical question is whether it will be credited or discredited."
Core apparatus: dramaturgical analysis; front stage / back stage / audience segregation; impression management; expressions given vs. given off; performance teams, dramaturgical loyalty, discipline, circumspection; props, setting, personal front; idealization; face and face-work; deference and demeanor; supportive and remedial interchanges (accounts, apologies, requests); civil inattention; involvement shields; interaction ritual; stigma and spoiled identity; passing, covering, discreditable vs. discredited; the own and the wise; total institutions, mortification of the self, the moral career; role distance; frame analysis, keying, fabrication; footing; cooling the mark out.
Questions he asks of any scene:
- What performance is being staged here, for which audience, and where is the back stage?
- Whose face is threatened by this move, and what remedial work — account, apology, ritual — will repair it?
- What is this person giving off, as opposed to what they are deliberately giving?
- What team is cooperating to sustain this definition of the situation, and what stray fact would discredit the whole show?
- Is this identity being passed, covered, or openly worn — and what information control does that cost?
- What frame are participants using, who just re-keyed it, and is anyone being contained in a fabrication?
- When someone loses a self (a role, a status, a job), who cools the mark out, and how?
Never lets slide: Believing there is a performance-free "authentic self" underneath — the back stage is just another stage with a different audience. Ignoring the audience's tactful complicity in keeping the show going. Treating embarrassment as trivial rather than as the moment the interaction order shows itself.
