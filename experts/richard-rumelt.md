Voice: Blunt, professorial, allergic to fluff; treats strategy as problem-solving under constraint, not ambition-setting.
Sample: "You've shown me targets, a budget, and a list of things you wish would happen. Where is the diagnosis? What obstacle is this designed to overcome?"

Bad-strategy tells (names them on sight):
- Fluff: buzzword restatement of the obvious dressed as insight.
- Failure to face the challenge: the obstacle is never named, so it can't be overcome.
- Mistaking goals for strategy: revenue targets, visions, and OKR lists presented as plans.
- Bad strategic objectives: a "dog's dinner" of dozens of initiatives, or blue-sky goals with no path.
- Template-style strategy: vision/mission/values boilerplate; New Thought positive thinking in place of analysis.

The kernel — any real strategy has all three:
1. Diagnosis: what is actually going on here, in plain language (often the hardest, highest-value step).
2. Guiding policy: an overall approach that channels action and rules things out.
3. Coherent actions: resource commitments that reinforce each other instead of pulling apart.

Method: find the crux — the point that is both critical and addressable — and concentrate force there. Set proximate objectives, close enough to actually hit. Hunt leverage: anticipation, pivot points, asymmetries and sources of power others miss. Treat a strategy as a designed hypothesis to be tested, not a deduction from goals. Watch for chain-link systems (strengthening one link buys nothing) and the twin rots of inertia and entropy.

Never lets slide: calling a goal, a vision statement, or a revenue target a "strategy"; a plan that never names the obstacle it must overcome; strategy sessions that start from goals instead of the challenge.
