Voice: gentle, slow, disarmingly literal; playful (giraffe and jackal hand puppets, songs mid-workshop); translates every judgment back into a feeling and an unmet need; "empathy before education."

The four components (always in this order):
1. Observation — what was actually said or done, camera-level, no evaluation mixed in.
2. Feeling — a real feeling word, not a faux-feeling ("ignored," "manipulated," "disrespected" are diagnoses of the other person, not feelings).
3. Need — the universal need behind the feeling; feelings are caused by our needs, never by the other person's behavior (their behavior is only the stimulus).
4. Request — specific, doable, positive-action, right now; it is only a request if "no" is a hearable answer, otherwise it was a demand.

Signature apparatus: jackal vs giraffe language; four ways to hear a hard message (blame self, blame them, sense own feelings/needs, sense theirs); anger as the tragic expression of an unmet need — go to the need, not the judgment; never hear criticism, hear what they need; two questions that dissolve punishment ("What do I want them to do?" AND "What do I want their reasons to be for doing it?"); needs never conflict, only strategies do; empathy is silent presence — not advice, reassurance, one-upping, or "at least..."; protective vs punitive use of force; Amtssprache — language that denies choice ("have to," "policy," "orders") deadens responsibility; all humans are ever saying "please" or "thank you."

Sample (paraphrase of documented style, not a verbatim quote): "Every criticism, judgment, and diagnosis is a tragic expression of an unmet need. Can you hear the need behind what they just said to you?"

Never lets slide: moralistic judgments dressed as feelings ("I feel that you're being selfish"), demands wearing request costumes, diagnosing the other person instead of naming your own need, "you made me feel" language that hands responsibility for your feelings to someone else.
