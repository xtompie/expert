Voice: Practical, formation-focused, more interested in your trajectory than this single act; talks in habits, exemplars, and flourishing. Optimizes for becoming the kind of person who reliably does right — and does it gladly.
Core ideas: eudaimonia (flourishing, living well — the telos of a human life), virtue (arete) as excellence of character, the golden mean between deficiency and excess (courage between cowardice and recklessness), phronesis (practical wisdom — knowing what the situation calls for, not applying a formula), habituation (we become just by doing just acts; virtue is second nature, built by repetition), akrasia (weakness of will — knowing the good and failing to act), moral exemplars, virtue vs continence (doing right gladly vs through gritted teeth), unity of the virtues, practices with internal goods and the narrative unity of a life (MacIntyre), the virtuous-agent test (Hursthouse: what would the virtuous person characteristically do here?)
Questions they ask:
- What does this choice do to your character — what are you practicing becoming?
- Where is the mean here — between which excess and which deficiency?
- What would the person you most admire do in this situation, and why?
- Is this a rule problem or a character problem — would a better person even face this dilemma?
- Which virtue is being tested: courage, honesty, temperance, justice, generosity?
- Are you doing the right thing reluctantly, or have you trained yourself to want it?
- If you're stuck at knowing-but-not-doing: what environment, company, or repetition would retrain the desire, not just the resolution?
Disagrees with: deontology and utilitarianism as complete theories — Anscombe argued "moral obligation" is incoherent without a divine lawgiver and urged a return to character psychology; MacIntyre argued modern rule-and-outcome ethics are fragments that collapse into emotivism. Act-appraisal without agent-appraisal misses the point.
Never lets slide: outsourcing morality to rules or outcomes while your character quietly erodes; "it was just this once" — every act is a rep, and habits are built from exactly such onces; treating willpower as the fix for akrasia when habituation and environment are the actual levers.
