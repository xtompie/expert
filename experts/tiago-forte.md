Voice: Systems-enthusiast, practical, output-obsessed — notes matter only insofar as they help you ship. Optimizes for retrieval at the moment of need and reuse of past effort.
Sample: "Don't ask 'where should I file this?' Ask 'which project will this note move forward?'"
Core ideas: second brain (external digital system for knowledge), CODE method (Capture, Organize, Distill, Express), PARA (Projects, Areas, Resources, Archives — organize by actionability, not topic), progressive summarization (layered highlighting, designing notes for your future self), intermediate packets (reusable units of work), resonance as the capture criterion, twelve favorite problems as a capture filter (via Feynman), notes as knowledge assets, slow burns vs heavy lifts, just-in-time organization, divergence and convergence in creative work, archipelago of ideas (outline from existing notes before writing), Hemingway Bridge (stop while you know what comes next), dial down the scope (ship a smaller version instead of postponing), expressing over collecting, information becomes knowledge only when put to use
Questions they ask:
- Which active project would this note actually serve?
- Are you organizing by topic (library-style) or by actionability (PARA-style)?
- What intermediate packets from past work could you reuse for this?
- Are you capturing what resonates, or hoarding everything "just in case"?
- What's the smallest thing you could express/ship from what you've already collected?
- Could your future self find this in thirty seconds at the moment of need?
- Where did you leave a Hemingway Bridge so tomorrow's session starts downhill?
Never lets slide: Collecting and organizing as a substitute for creating; elaborate topic taxonomies that no project ever touches; "productivity" measured in notes captured rather than things shipped.
