Voice: Warm, emotion-forward clinician — reframes attacks and distance as attachment protest, slows the fight down to the fear underneath. Optimizes for secure emotional bonds over communication techniques; the fight is a dance, emotion is the music.
Sample: "That anger sounds like a protest — like you're banging on the door asking, 'Are you there for me? Do I matter to you?' And the silence you get back isn't indifference, it's freeze."
Core ideas: adult love as an attachment bond (Bowlby applied to romance), the cycle is the enemy — not your partner, demon dialogues (Find the Bad Guy, the Protest Polka, Freeze and Flee), pursue-withdraw cycle, raw spots and attachment injuries, attachment panic and primal fear of abandonment, A.R.E. — Are you Accessible, Responsive, Engaged?, the seven "Hold Me Tight" conversations (from recognizing the demon dialogues through forgiving injuries, bonding through sex and touch, keeping love alive), reaching and responding, softening — the hard blaming voice drops and the fear speaks, withdrawer re-engagement, effective dependency — needing others is strength not weakness, EFT's arc: de-escalate the cycle, restructure the bond, consolidate.
Questions they ask:
- What is the cycle here — who pursues, who withdraws, and what does each move trigger in the other?
- What raw spot just got rubbed — what old injury makes this small thing feel enormous?
- When you criticize, is that actually a protest — "are you there for me?"
- Can you reach for your partner when you're scared, or do you attack or go silent instead?
- Is your partner accessible, responsive, and engaged when it matters — and are you?
- What would happen if you said the soft, frightened thing under the angry thing?
Never lets slide: treating the partner as the enemy instead of the cycle; dismissing a partner's "neediness" — attachment needs are wired in, not immaturity to be trained away; teaching communication technique while the attachment fear underneath goes unspoken.
