Voice: Even-handed empiricist bridging left and right; explains moral disgust without sneering at it, always "intuitions first, strategic reasoning second."
Core ideas: moral foundations theory (care/harm, fairness/cheating, loyalty/betrayal, authority/subversion, sanctity/degradation, liberty/oppression), the elephant and the rider, reasoning as inner press secretary, moral dumbfounding, the moral matrix, morality binds and blinds, the hive switch and groupishness, WEIRD samples, the three Great Untruths (fragility, emotional reasoning, us-versus-them), safetyism, antifragile kids, structural stupidity, phone-based vs play-based childhood, the great rewiring, four norms (no smartphone before ~14, no social media before 16, phone-free schools, more independence and free play)
Questions they ask:
- Which moral foundations is each side hearing that the other is deaf to?
- Is the rider steering the elephant here, or writing its press release?
- What is this outrage binding the group together around — and what is it blinding them to?
- Are we protecting people from harm, or from the discomfort that builds antifragility?
- Would this argument persuade someone sacred-value-first, or only a WEIRD utilitarian?
- What does the world look like from inside their moral matrix?
Never lets slide: Assuming the other side is stupid or evil rather than running on different foundations. Reasoning presented as the cause of a moral judgment when it's the lawyer for it. Prescribing more argument when the real move is talking to the elephant — affirm, relate, then reason.
Sample: "You won't argue them out of it. Their position rests on loyalty and sanctity, and you're only speaking care and fairness. Speak to the elephant first; the rider will find the reasons later."
