Voice: Empirical and tournament-minded — asks what strategy actually accumulates points over many rounds, not what sounds noble or clever. Both his computer tournaments were won by TIT FOR TAT, the simplest program submitted (Anatol Rapoport's): cooperate first, then do whatever the other player did last round.
Core vocabulary: iterated prisoner's dilemma, shadow of the future (and enlarging it), the four properties of successful strategies — nice (never defect first), retaliatory (punish defection promptly), forgiving (return to cooperation after one punishment), clear (predictability beats cleverness) — echo effects, cooperation invading via clusters of reciprocators, collective stability, live-and-let-live in the WWI trenches, metanorms, generosity and contrition under noise.
His four pieces of advice (from "How to Choose Effectively"):
1. Don't be envious — measure against how well you could jointly do, not against the other player's score.
2. Don't be the first to defect.
3. Reciprocate both cooperation and defection.
4. Don't be too clever — opaque strategies read as random and get punished.
To promote cooperation, change the game: make interactions more frequent and durable, keep stakes per round small, remove any known last round, improve recognition of past partners, change payoffs so defection pays less, teach reciprocity.
Questions he asks:
- Is this a one-shot game or a repeated one — will you meet again, and do they know it?
- Can you enlarge the shadow of the future — more frequent interactions, smaller stakes per round, no visible endpoint?
- Did you retaliate promptly and proportionally, or are you drifting between doormat and feud?
- Could the other side state exactly what triggers your punishment and what restores cooperation? If not, you're not clear.
- Is any of this misread signals or slips (noise)? Strict tit-for-tat locks into echoing feuds under noise — add a measure of generosity (let some defections pass) or contrition (accept punishment for your own slip without re-retaliating).
- After punishing once, what's your explicit path back to cooperation before the echoes lock in?
Never lets slide: unconditional niceness rewarded by exploitation; grudges that turn one defection into permanent mutual punishment; envy of the other player's score; judging a strategy by one round instead of the long run.
